# Pack 13 plan: Wild Frontiers

Status: **decided by Claude with the owner's trust** (10/31/2026: "i trust you"; "program all 9 packs 1 at a time";
11/01/2026: "go ahead" for Pack 13, the last of the nine). The design (`GAME_DESIGN.md`: Fantasy realms) names each
realm, its source and one boss idea; everything else was chosen by Claude from old public-domain texts only. The owner
can change or veto any of it. Mount Pelion built and released 11/01/2026 (00.03.26); Dovrefjell (00.03.27); the City of Brass (00.03.28); the Sea King's Hall (00.03.29). **All four realms are built.**

Realms work like Packs 5-12: endless map, a hazard, monster families, three bosses, no local gods.

## How it is built
- One realm at a time: Mount Pelion, Dovrefjell, the City of Brass, the Sea King's Hall. Then two secrets per realm.
- **Heading:** "Pack 13: Wild Frontiers".
- **Unlock (Claude's choice):** each realm opens from a Pack 12 30:00 boss: Finvarra → Mount Pelion, the Flying
  Dutchman → Dovrefjell, the Shadow → the City of Brass, the Spriggan → the Sea King's Hall.
- **Difficulty:** as Packs 4-12 (+60% enemy HP, +35% spawns, +30% damage).
- **Heroes:** none.
- **Care:** Mount Pelion is the wild centaurs' country (Ovid, Apollodorus), not the Arcadian wilds; Chiron, the wise
  centaur, is not an enemy. Dovrefjell keeps to Ibsen's play (1867) and Asbjørnsen and Moe's tales, not later versions.
  The City of Brass uses only the tale's brass horseman, its automatons and its desert ruins: the jinn and the
  religious framing of the tale are left out (the design's note). The Sea King's Hall is Andersen's (1837), not any
  film's; the Little Mermaid, her sisters and the Sea King's family are not enemies.

## Realm 49: Mount Pelion (the centaurs' mountain): **done and released 11/01/2026 (00.03.26)**
*Sources: Ovid, Metamorphoses 9.98-133 (Nessus carries Deianira over the river Evenus; Heracles shoots him from the far
bank with an arrow dipped in the Hydra's venom; his poisoned blood) and 12.210-535 (the battle of the Lapiths and the
centaurs at Pirithous' wedding: Eurytus, who seized the bride and was struck down with an old wine-bowl; Rhoetus, who
snatched a burning brand from the altar; the centaurs tearing up trees and rocks to throw); Apollodorus 2.5.4, 2.7.6.*

**Hazard: the uprooted trees.** The centaurs tear up trees and boulders and hurl them down the slopes (telegraphed
strikes, the rockslide look in wood and stone).

## Realm 50: Dovrefjell (the trolls' mountain): **done and released 11/01/2026 (00.03.27)**
*Sources: Henrik Ibsen, Peer Gynt (1867; not later versions): the hall of the Dovre-King, his trolls and their tails,
the troll motto "Troll, to thyself be enough", the Great Bøyg in the dark that says "Go round about", and the church
bells that save Peer as the hall falls; Peter Christen Asbjørnsen and Jørgen Moe, Norwegian Folktales (1841-44): "The
Boys Who Met the Trolls in the Hedal Woods" (three trolls with one eye between them).*

**Hazard: the trolls' boulders.** Trolls hurl boulders down the fells (telegraphed strikes, the rockslide look).

## Realm 51: the City of Brass: **done and released 11/01/2026 (00.03.28)**
*Source: "The City of Brass" in the One Thousand and One Nights (Richard Burton's translation, 1885, nights 566-578):
the long desert road, the brass horseman on a pillar whose spear points the way when he is turned, the walled city with
no gate whose watchers on the wall threw themselves down, and the embalmed queen guarded by two automatons that strike
down anyone who takes her jewels.* Only the automatons, the horseman and the desert ruins are used: the jinn in the
tale come from religious belief and are left out (the design's note).

**Hazard: the sandstorm.** A desert storm sweeps over the ruins, pushing the Heroes and closing the view (the weather
veil, sand, with a push).

## Realm 52: the Sea King's Hall: **done and released 11/01/2026 (00.03.29)**
*Source: Hans Christian Andersen, "The Little Mermaid" (1837; not any film): the Sea King's palace of coral and amber
at the bottom of the sea, the storm that wrecked the prince's ship, the Sea Witch's house of drowned men's bones in a
wood of polyps "half animal and half plant" that seize whatever comes near and never let go, the fat water-snakes she
called her little chickens, and the potion she brewed in her cauldron.*

**Hazard: the deep currents.** The sea's currents sweep the Heroes along and the deep water closes in (the weather
veil, sea-blue, with a push and Soak).
