# Pack 5 plan: Gothic Night

Status: **decided by Claude with the owner's trust** (10/31/2026: "i trust you"; "program all 9 packs 1 at a time").
The design (`GAME_DESIGN.md`: Fantasy realms) names each realm, its source and one boss idea; everything else below was
chosen by Claude from old public-domain texts and folklore only (never modern games, books or films). The owner can
change or veto any of it, before or after it is built. Erebus built and released 10/31/2026 (00.02.85); Karnstein (00.02.86); Ossory (00.02.87); the Brocken (00.02.88). **All four realms are built.** Baubo reuses the Wild Hunt's ride (she charges on her sow and calls two witches each time).

Realms work like Pack 4's: endless map, a hazard, monster families, three bosses, **no local gods** (shrines draw gods
from every pantheon).

## How it is built
- One realm at a time: Erebus, Karnstein, Ossory, the Brocken. Then two secrets per realm, banter and lore check rows.
- **Heading:** "Pack 5: Gothic Night" in the level select (after the finale).
- **Unlock (Claude's choice):** each realm opens from a Pack 4 30:00 boss: Níðhöggr → Erebus (the dead of Náströnd),
  Dahut → Karnstein, Sinmara → Ossory, the Roc → the Brocken.
- **Difficulty (Claude's choice):** the same as Pack 4 (+60% enemy HP, +35% spawns, +30% damage), so the Crossroads
  stays the hardest level and the packs after it don't keep climbing. The later packs (6-13) use the same.
- **Heroes:** none (the design's post-launch Hero list has none from these stories).

## Realm 17: Erebus (the restless dead): **done and released 10/31/2026 (00.02.85)**
*Sources: Hesiod, Theogony 123 (Erebus, the darkness), 211-217 (the Keres); Aristophanes, The Frogs 285-295 (Empusa);
Pausanias, Description of Greece 10.28.7 (Eurynomos, in Polygnotus' painting of the underworld); the Anglo-Saxon
Chronicle for 1127 (the hunt seen at Peterborough); Orderic Vitalis, Ecclesiastical History 8.17 (Hellequin's
household, 1091); Jacob Grimm, Teutonic Mythology ch. 31 (the furious host).*

**Hazard: the grasping dead.** Graves open in the dark ground and hands reach up: patches that slow and hurt whoever
stands in them, until they sink back (the bramble patches, drawn as graves).

| Family | What it does | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shades | Swarms, flyers and encirclers (already in the game) | Odyssey 11 |
| Keres | Dark-winged death spirits, fast flyers | Theogony 211-217; Iliad 18.535 |
| Hounds of the Hunt | Black hounds with burning eyes, fast chasers | Anglo-Saxon Chronicle 1127 (black hounds with wide eyes) |

| Time | Boss | Mechanic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10:00 | **Empusa** | The shape-shifter of the dark road: "now a bull, now a mule, now a lovely woman, now a dog" (The Frogs). Every few seconds she changes shape: the bull is armoured, the dog is fast, the woman casts |
| 20:00 | **Eurynomos** | The daemon who eats the flesh of the dead (Pausanias). Every few seconds he devours the monsters near him and heals for each; fight him away from the crowd |
| 30:00 | **The Wild Hunt** (the design's) | The black huntsmen on black horses with their hounds. Again and again the Hunt gallops straight across the arena at a Hero, leaving a trail of cold mist that slows, and looses its hounds each time it rides |

## Realm 18: Karnstein (the vampires): **done and released 10/31/2026 (00.02.86)**
*Sources: J. Sheridan Le Fanu, Carmilla (1872); John Polidori, The Vampyre (1819); James Malcolm Rymer and Thomas
Peckett Prest, Varney the Vampire (1845-47); Augustin Calmet, Treatise on the Apparitions of Spirits and on Vampires
(1746).*

**Hazard: the languor.** A pale night mist settles over the ruined estate; while it lies thick, every Hero slowly wastes
(the weather veil, with Poison), as Carmilla's victims did.

| Family | What it does | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenants | Slow, tough dead who rise from their graves | Calmet (1746) |
| Night bats | Swarms | Carmilla; Varney |
| Wolves | Chasers (already in the game) | Calmet; Polidori (the forest at night) |

| Time | Boss | Mechanic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10:00 | **Varney** | The vampire whom moonlight brings back (Varney the Vampire). The first time he falls he lies in the moonlight and rises again unless a Hero stands over him and blocks the light |
| 20:00 | **Lord Ruthven** | The cold nobleman of The Vampyre. He fastens on the nearest Hero and drains their life to heal himself while they stay close |
| 30:00 | **Carmilla** (the design's) | Mircalla, Countess Karnstein. She fades into mist and appears beside a Hero; her bite wastes (Poison). In her last phase she becomes the "monstrous cat", sooty black (Carmilla, ch. 6) |

## Realm 19: Ossory (the werewolf forests): **done and released 10/31/2026 (00.02.87)**
*Sources: Gerald of Wales, Topographia Hibernica II.19 (c. 1188: the werewolves of Ossory); Marie de France,
Bisclavret (12th c.); the Lai de Melion (13th c.); French newspapers of 1764-67 and the Abbé Pourcher's history (1889)
on the Beast of Gévaudan. **Care:** the saint who cursed the people of Ossory is not used as a character.*

**Hazard: the full moon.** The moon comes out over the forest; while it shines, every monster runs faster (the weather
veil, faint silver, with a monster speed boost).

| Family | What it does | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Werewolves of Ossory | Wolf-shaped men, brutes | Gerald of Wales |
| Wolves | Packs (already in the game) | Gerald of Wales; Melion (the pack he leads) |
| Ravens | Flyers over the forest | Irish forest; wolves' companions in old texts |

| Time | Boss | Mechanic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10:00 | **Bisclavret** | The baron who becomes a wolf and needs his hidden clothes to turn back (Marie de France). His clothes lie in the arena: stand on them and he turns back into a man for a few seconds, unable to fight and hurt twice as hard |
| 20:00 | **Melion** | The knight left a wolf, who led a pack of wolves through Ireland (Lai de Melion). He calls wolves, and each wolf near him shields him |
| 30:00 | **The Beast of Gévaudan** (the design's) | The great wolf-like beast of 1764-67. It vanishes into the trees and pounces out where the undergrowth stirs |

## Realm 20: the Brocken (the witches' mountain): **done and released 10/31/2026 (00.02.88)**
*Sources: Goethe, Faust Part One (1808), the Walpurgis Night scene (the witches' ride up the Brocken; the will-o'-the-
wisp who guides Faust; old Baubo on her sow); Johannes Praetorius, Blockes-Berges Verrichtung (1668); Johann
Silberschlag (1780) on the Brocken spectre; Leicestershire folklore of Black Annis and her bower in the Dane Hills
(recorded by John Heyrick, 1797, and county histories).*

**Hazard: the witches' ride.** On Walpurgis Night the witches fly over the mountain and loose hexes: telegraphed strikes
that Hex whoever they hit (weaker armour and damage for a while).

| Family | What it does | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Witches | Casters on broomsticks | Faust (Walpurgis Night); Praetorius |
| Will-o'-the-wisps | Flyers (already in the game) | Faust (the Irrlicht guides Faust up the Brocken) |
| Black goats | Charging beasts the witches ride | Praetorius; Faust |

| Time | Boss | Mechanic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10:00 | **The Brocken Spectre** | The giant shadow seen in the Brocken's mist (Silberschlag, 1780). It always stands opposite you across the arena, like a shadow: walk toward the middle to reach it |
| 20:00 | **Baubo** | "Old Baubo comes alone; she rides a farrow sow" (Faust). She charges on her sow and calls witches after her |
| 30:00 | **Black Annis** (the design's) | The blue-faced hag with iron claws who dug her bower in the Dane Hills. She hides in her bower to heal, then springs out at the nearest Hero; her claws make wounds that bleed (Poison) |
