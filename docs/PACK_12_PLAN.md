# Pack 12 plan: Otherworlds

Status: **decided by Claude with the owner's trust** (10/31/2026: "i trust you"; "program all 9 packs 1 at a time";
11/01/2026: "go ahead" for Pack 12). The design (`GAME_DESIGN.md`: Fantasy realms) names each realm, its source and one
boss idea; everything else was chosen by Claude from old public-domain texts only. The owner can change or veto any of
it. Tír na nÓg built and released 11/01/2026 (00.03.21); Hy-Brasil (00.03.22); the Shadow's Court (00.03.23).

Realms work like Packs 5-11: endless map, a hazard, monster families, three bosses, no local gods.

## How it is built
- One realm at a time: Tír na nÓg, Hy-Brasil, the Shadow's Court, the Knockers' Deep. Then two secrets per realm.
- **Heading:** "Pack 12: Otherworlds".
- **Unlock (Claude's choice):** each realm opens from a Pack 11 30:00 boss: the Lord of Ashkarrow → Tír na nÓg, the
  White Dragon → Hy-Brasil, Atlas → the Shadow's Court, the Herald of the Void → the Knockers' Deep.
- **Difficulty:** as Packs 4-11 (+60% enemy HP, +35% spawns, +30% damage).
- **Heroes:** none.
- **Care:** Tír na nÓg keeps to the 18th-century Irish lay and 19th-century collected folklore; Oisín and Niamh, the
  heroes of the tale, are not enemies. The Flying Dutchman is the sailors' legend of 1795-1821, not any film or opera.
  The Shadow is Andersen's (1847). The Knockers' Deep keeps to Hunt's and Bottrell's Cornish collections.

## Realm 45: Tír na nÓg (the Land of Youth): **done and released 11/01/2026 (00.03.21)**
*Sources: Mícheál Coimín, "Laoi Oisín i dTír na nÓg" (c. 1750; the lay of Oisín in the Land of Youth): Niamh's white
horse over the sea, the hornless fawn chased by a white hound with red ears, the Fomorian giant Oisín fought for three
days and nights; Lady Wilde, Ancient Legends of Ireland (1887), "Ethna the Bride" (Finvarra, king of the fairies of
the West, carries off Ethna into his hill of Knockma; her husband digs into the hill to win her back) and the fairy
whirlwind; W. B. Yeats, Fairy and Folk Tales of the Irish Peasantry (1888).*

**Hazard: the fairy wind.** The sídhe pass in a whirlwind: a strong wind sweeps the Heroes along and closes the view
(the weather veil, golden, with a strong push).

| Family | What it does | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sídhe warriors | Chasers | Yeats; Lady Wilde |
| Sídhe pipers | Casters (the fairy music) | Lady Wilde |
| Will-o'-the-wisps | Flyers (already in the game) | folklore |
| The sluagh | Swarms (already in the game) | folklore |

| Time | Boss | Mechanic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10:00 | **The Red-Eared Hound** | The white hound with red ears that forever chases a hornless fawn. The fawn runs about the arena; a Hero who reaches it first leaves the hound baffled (dazed, double damage) |
| 20:00 | **The Fomor** | The giant Oisín fought for three days and three nights. By day he fights; at each nightfall he rests and gets back a third of the damage done to him since dawn |
| 30:00 | **Finvarra** (the design's) | The fairy king of Knockma. He carries a Hero off into his hill for a moment (whisked away across the arena and Hexed). His hill stands in the arena: Heroes who dig into it (stand on it) break his guard, as Ethna's husband did |

## Realm 46: Hy-Brasil (the isle seen once in seven years): **done and released 11/01/2026 (00.03.22)**
*Sources: Roderick O'Flaherty, A Chorographical Description of West or H-Iar Connaught (1684: O'Brasil, the
enchanted island sometimes seen off the coast); "O-Brazile, or the Inchanted Island" (1675 pamphlet: Captain John
Nisbet's crew land, find great black rabbits and a castle, and light a fire on the shore, which breaks the spell a
necromancer laid on the island); the Flying Dutchman: George Barrington, A Voyage to Botany Bay (1795), and
"Vanderdecken's Message Home", Blackwood's Magazine (May 1821: the Dutch captain who swore he would round the Cape if
it took till doomsday, his ship seen in storms, his crew asking passing ships to carry letters home to the long
dead).*

**Hazard: the mist of seven years.** Thick sea mist hides the island (the weather veil, grey-white).

| Family | What it does | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Black rabbits | Swarms ("great black rabbits") | the 1675 pamphlet |
| The Dutchman's crew | Ghost sailors, chasers | Blackwood's, 1821 |
| Drowned sailors | Brutes (already in the game: the drowned of Ys) | |
| Storm gusts | Flyers (already in the game) | the storm off the Cape |

| Time | Boss | Mechanic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10:00 | **The Great Black Rabbit** | The biggest of the island's black rabbits: it bounds from spot to spot (marked first), landing hard |
| 20:00 | **The Necromancer of O'Brazile** | His spell holds the island, and while it holds he can't be hurt much. Three piles of driftwood lie on the shore: a Hero who lights one (stands at it) weakens the spell; with all three lit it breaks (his guard breaks). They burn out after a while |
| 30:00 | **The Flying Dutchman** (the design's) | Vanderdecken's ship. By turns it is a phantom (it can't be hurt, sailing across the arena and striking whoever it passes) and a solid ship, hove to, firing on marked spots. Its crew send letters for the long dead drifting after the Heroes: a Hero one reaches is Hexed for a while |

## Realm 47: the Shadow's Court: **done and released 11/01/2026 (00.03.23)**
*Source: Hans Christian Andersen, "The Shadow" (1847; not any later version): the learned man's shadow goes into the
house opposite, where Poetry lives, comes back years later as a rich man, makes his old master into his shadow, marries
a princess who suffers from seeing too clearly, and has the learned man put to death.*

**Hazard: the lengthening shadows.** Evening falls and the shadows stretch: the view closes in (the weather veil, dusk).

| Family | What it does | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Living shadows | Chasers | Andersen |
| Lamp moths | Swarms round the lamps | the house opposite |
| Night bats | Flyers (already in the game) | |

| Time | Boss | Mechanic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10:00 | **The Learned Man** | The master who became his shadow's shadow. He clings to the dark side of the nearest Hero, away from the lamp in the arena; brought close to the lamp's light he fades and takes double damage |
| 20:00 | **The Princess Who Saw Too Clearly** | Her sight is too sharp: her gaze sweeps round her in a wide cone, hurting and Shocking every Hero it falls on. She can't see behind her: blows from behind hurt her more |
| 30:00 | **The Shadow** (the design's) | He who became his master's master. He tears the shadows off the Heroes: each Hero's shadow rises as a monster and hunts its owner, and while any stolen shadow stands, he takes half damage |

## Realm 48: the Knockers' Deep
*Sources: Robert Hunt, Popular Romances of the West of England (1865: the knockers who knock where the rich lodes lie,
and must be left a bit of the miner's pasty; the spriggans who guard the treasure buried in the cairns and can swell
to a giant's size; the piskies who lead people astray, and "Jack o' the lantern, Joan the wad"); William Bottrell,
Traditions and Hearthside Stories of West Cornwall (1870-73).*

**Hazard: the cave-ins.** Rock falls from the roof of the old workings (telegraphed strikes, the rockslide look in
dark stone).

| Family | What it does | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spriggans | Ugly little treasure guards, chasers | Hunt |
| Knockers | Swarms | Hunt |
| Piskies | Flyers | Hunt |

| Time | Boss | Mechanic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10:00 | **The Old Knocker** | He knocks on marked spots, and the roof comes down there. Leave him his share: crusts of pasty appear, and a Hero who takes one pleases him (he stops knocking for a while and lets his guard down: double damage) |
| 20:00 | **Joan the Wad** | The piskie with the torch. Her light leads the farthest Hero astray: they are drawn after it for a moment |
| 30:00 | **The Spriggan** (the design's) | The treasure's guard. Every so often he swells to a giant's size (slow, hitting hard, hard to hurt, stamping out rings), then shrinks again, spent (double damage). Gold lies in his cairn: a Hero who takes some (run gold) makes him swell at once |
