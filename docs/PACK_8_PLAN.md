# Pack 8 plan: Warlords

Status: **decided by Claude with the owner's trust** (10/31/2026: "i trust you"; "program all 9 packs 1 at a time";
11/01/2026: "go ahead" for Pack 8). The design (`GAME_DESIGN.md`: Fantasy realms) names each realm, its source and one
boss idea; everything else was chosen by Claude from old public-domain texts only. Ghorvast built and released 11/01/2026 (00.03.01). The owner can change or veto any of
it.

Realms work like Packs 5-7: endless map, a hazard, monster families, three bosses, no local gods.

## How it is built
- One realm at a time: Ghorvast, Knossos, Utgard, Logres. Then two secrets per realm.
- **Heading:** "Pack 8: Warlords".
- **Unlock (Claude's choice):** each realm opens from a Pack 7 30:00 boss: the Hydra → Ghorvast, Lycaon → Knossos, the
  Erlking → Utgard, the Queen of Elfame → Logres.
- **Difficulty:** as Packs 4-7 (+60% enemy HP, +35% spawns, +30% damage).
- **Heroes:** none.
- **Keeping apart from the myth levels** (the design's note): Knossos is the Labyrinth and Minos' court, not the Aegean
  shore; Utgard is Útgarða-Loki's illusions and rigged contests, not Jotunheim's mountains; Logres is Vortigern's
  kingdom, not Avalon.

## Realm 29: Ghorvast (the orc stronghold) — invented: **done and released 11/01/2026 (00.03.01)**
*The design marks this realm invented: "orc" itself is an old public word (Old English* orcneas*, Latin* Orcus*), but no
city lore exists. Everything in it has plain descriptive names, for the owner to approve or rename. Nothing is taken
from modern fantasy (no wargs, no named orc breeds).*

**Hazard: fire-arrow volleys.** Volleys of burning arrows fall from the walls (telegraphed strikes that Burn).

## Realm 30: Knossos (the Labyrinth city)
*Sources: Apollodorus, Library 2.5.7 (the Cretan Bull) and 3.1.3-4, 3.15.8, Epitome 1.7-9 (Minos, Pasiphaë, the
Labyrinth; Theseus kills the Minotaur with his fists); Plutarch, Life of Theseus 15-19; Homer, Odyssey 11.568 (Minos
judging the dead); Ovid, Metamorphoses 8.152-182.*

**Hazard: the Labyrinth's dark.** In the twisting halls the light fails and the view closes in (the weather veil).
