# Pack 11 plan: The Abyss

Status: **decided by Claude with the owner's trust** (10/31/2026: "i trust you"; "program all 9 packs 1 at a time";
11/01/2026: "go ahead" for Pack 11). The design (`GAME_DESIGN.md`: Fantasy realms) names each realm, its source and one
boss idea; everything else was chosen by Claude from old public-domain texts only. The owner can change or veto any of
it. Ashkarrow built and released 11/01/2026 (00.03.16); Dinas Emrys (00.03.17); Atlantis (00.03.18).

Realms work like Packs 5-10: endless map, a hazard, monster families, three bosses, no local gods.

## How it is built
- One realm at a time: Ashkarrow, Dinas Emrys, Atlantis, Ginnungagap. Then two secrets per realm.
- **Heading:** "Pack 11: The Abyss".
- **Unlock (Claude's choice):** each realm opens from a Pack 10 30:00 boss: the Griffin Matriarch → Ashkarrow, Orion →
  Dinas Emrys, the Snow Queen → Atlantis, Phobetor → Ginnungagap.
- **Difficulty:** as Packs 4-10 (+60% enemy HP, +35% spawns, +30% damage).
- **Heroes:** none.
- **Care:** Ashkarrow is invented because public demon lore is religious (the design): nothing in it comes from any
  religion, and every name is plain and descriptive. At Dinas Emrys the Red Dragon (the Welsh national symbol) never
  appears; the boss is the White Dragon (the design's note). Atlantis is Plato's, not any film's; its Atlas is Plato's
  first king, not the Titan. Ginnungagap is the Eddas' void; Ymir is already in the game (Jotunheim), so he is not here.

## Realm 42: Dinas Emrys (the dragons' hill): **done and released 11/01/2026 (00.03.17)**
*Sources: Historia Brittonum (9th c.), 40-42 (Vortigern's tower on the hill, the pool beneath, the two dragons that
fight, the white one driven off); "Lludd and Llefelys" (Welsh, in the Red Book of Hergest, c. 1400; public domain in
Lady Charlotte Guest's translation, 1849): the three plagues of Britain — the Coraniaid, who heard every word the wind
carried; the dragons' scream every May Eve, which took men's strength away; and the magician who lulled the court
asleep with music and stole the feast — the dragons caught in a vat of mead under a silk cloth, drunk and asleep, and
buried at Dinas Emrys. Llefelys killed the Coraniaid with water steeped with insects, harmless to the Britons.*
The Red Dragon never appears.

**Hazard: the May-Eve scream.** The dragons scream above Britain: the air shivers and every Hero loses strength (the
weather veil, pale, with Hex).

| Men-at-arms | Vortigern's soldiers, chasers (already in the game) | Historia Brittonum 40 |
| Wyrmlings | Little dragons, swarms (the "worms" of the pool) | Historia Brittonum 42 |
| Ravens | Flyers (already in the game) | |

| Time | Boss | Mechanic |
| --- | --- | --- |

## Realm 43: Atlantis (the sunken city): **done and released 11/01/2026 (00.03.18)**
*Sources: Plato, Timaeus 24e-25d (Atlantis, beyond the Pillars of Heracles, swallowed by the sea in a single day and
night of earthquakes and floods) and Critias 113b-121c (Poseidon and Cleito; the rings of sea and land round the
island; the twin kings Atlas and Gadeirus; the walls coated in brass, tin and orichalcum; the army's heavy infantry,
slingers and archers; the bulls hunted "without weapons, with staves and nooses" at the pillar of orichalcum).* Atlas is
Plato's first king, not the Titan. Poseidon, a god in the game, is not a boss.

**Hazard: the sinking.** Earthquakes shake the island and the sea floods in (the flood bands, slowing).

| Family | What it does | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Atlantean hoplites | Heavy infantry, chasers | Critias 119a |
| Atlantean slingers | Casters | Critias 119b |
| Gulls | Swarms (already in the game) | the sea |
| Drowned sailors | Brutes (already in the game: the drowned of Ys) | the sunken city |

| Time | Boss | Mechanic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10:00 | **Poseidon's Bull** | The sacred bull the kings hunted with staves and nooses. It charges; nooses lie in the arena, and a Hero who takes one ropes it: it is held fast and takes double damage |
| 20:00 | **Gadeirus** | Atlas' twin, king of the land toward the Pillars of Heracles. He raises Poseidon's rings of sea round himself: rings of water spread out from him, hurting and Soaking whoever they pass |
| 30:00 | **Atlas** (the design's; Plato's king) | He stands behind the city's three walls — brass, tin and orichalcum — and each must be broken before he can be hurt. They rise again after a while. In his last phase the island sinks: the ground falls in under the Heroes (marked spots) |

## Realm 44: Ginnungagap (the yawning void)
*Sources: the Poetic Edda, Völuspá 3 ("there was a yawning gap, and grass nowhere") and Vafþrúðnismál 35 (Bergelmir
on his lúðr); Snorri, Gylfaginning 4-7 (Niflheim's rime and Muspelheim's fire meeting in the gap; the venom of the
Élivágar rivers hardening into rime; the cow Auðumbla licking the salty rime-blocks; the flood of Ymir's blood that
drowned all the frost giants but Bergelmir).* Ymir is already in the game, so he is not here.

**Hazard: the yawning gap.** The void opens under the ground (the chasms, in void colours).

| Family | What it does | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Venom rime | The Élivágar's venom, hardened to rime, chasers | Gylfaginning 5 |
| Rime drops | Swarms, the drops that quickened where rime met fire | Gylfaginning 5 |
| Muspell sparks | Bombers (already in the game) | Gylfaginning 4 |
| Frost thursar | Brutes (already in the game) | Gylfaginning 5 |

| Time | Boss | Mechanic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10:00 | **Auðumbla** | The first cow, who fed on the salty rime-blocks. She goes to lick them, and every lick heals her; break the blocks first by standing at them |
| 20:00 | **Bergelmir** | The one frost giant who lived through the flood of Ymir's blood, in his lúðr. Every so often the blood floods the arena (bands that slow) and he rides it in his lúðr, fast and out of reach |
| 30:00 | **The Herald of the Void** (the design's, invented: plain name) | He stands between rime and fire, turning from one to the other. In rime he Chills, in fire he Burns; a blow of the other element shatters his form (stunned, double damage). The gap opens under the farthest Hero |
